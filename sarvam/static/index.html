Methods and papers behind this

  • Hear2Act (Aug 2026): audio LLMs barely use prosody unless it is written down as explicit state. Used for the tone card shown under each message.
  • SimulU, simultaneous S2ST without aligned data, StreamSpeech: commit translation while the speaker is still talking. This app only borrows the idea (pause-triggered segments); it does not use those models.
  • Numbers guard: speech-translation models are weakest on names and numbers (Gaido et al., Seamless). Every message shows whether the same numbers were said and translated, with native digits (५०, ௫௦) normalized.
  • Read-back check: in a physician study, backtranslation helped catch more clinically harmful MT errors than quality scores alone, and current speech-translation metrics miss prosody and gender agreement, so the speaker gets to see what was heard.
  • Evidence-gated glossary: EGTA (Jul 2026) injects only terms with evidence in the current stream, and entity-aware metrics use fuzzy, alignment-free entity matching. Your terms bias Saaras and are enforced only when they are actually said.
  • Song mode: lyric lines are translated to the melody's note count with a measure-and-retry loop, as in MAVL / SylAVL-CoT (EMNLP 2025) (syllable constraints), and hums are matched to known tunes by pitch-contour warping, the classic baseline that learned hum embeddings improve on. Sung lyrics are harder to recognize than speech (VocalParse, 2026), hence the editable lyrics box. The "singing" is speech retimed onto the tune, not a trained singing voice.
  • AI Twin (CHI 2026): hearing speech back in your own voice was more motivating. Basis for the optional voice clone.
  • IndicFDB (Sep 2026): full-duplex voice-agent behaviours in Indian languages. Only the simplest form, barge-in, is implemented.
  • STEB: expressiveness benchmark for speech translation (Chinese-English). Not implemented; a checklist for evaluating this app.
